Problem Set 5 · individual

My agent: the Invoice Alert Agent

One Zap on Zapier’s free plan, with one trigger and one action. It watches a personal Gmail account for invoice emails and sends me an alert, so no supplier invoice sits unnoticed. All emails go to my own address, and addresses are cropped out of every screenshot.

Agent card

My agent card | Invoice Alert Agent | finished 20 October 2026, 7:30 PM
1. The job
Sends an alert email to the finance assistant (in this build, me) whenever an invoice email arrives, so no supplier invoice sits unnoticed.
2. The trigger
A new email arrives in my personal Gmail whose subject contains “invoice” (Gmail search: subject:invoice).
3. The tools
Gmail (New Email Matching Search, read only) and Email by Zapier (Send Outbound Email).
4. It must never
Send an alert to anyone but me. The “To” field is typed in as my own address, not mapped from the incoming email.
5. Where a person checks
I read each alert when it arrives and compare it with the original email in Gmail, and I check Zap History each evening.
Updated after the tricky case: trigger search changed to subject:(invoice OR inv), because the test email “Inv. #2041 due Friday” was missed.

The alert’s subject is “Invoice alert: ” plus the original subject, and its body lists the sender, date, subject and a preview, ending with “Please check the original email in Gmail before acting on it.” The tool menu has no reply, forward or delete, so the agent can only tell me; I decide what to do.

Q1. Where does my agent sit on the map, and what does that mean for the person in the loop?

(a) Quadrant. My Invoice Alert Agent sits in AI alone, with checks (bottom left). It is marked on the map above in teal.

(b) The two questions. First, this is work with a right answer. An email either has “invoice” in its subject or it doesn’t, and every alert can be checked as right or wrong against the original email. My runs showed this: in three runs, at 20:53, 21:15 and 21:2x, each alert named the right supplier (Rocky, john and Rocky) when I checked it against Zap History. One alert’s subject and first line came through garbled, and that too was plainly right or wrong to check. The tricky case showed it as well: the email “Inv. #2041 due Friday” was missed, which was clearly a wrong answer, not a matter of judgement. Second, this is work that either of them can do alone. A person could scan the inbox for invoices without AI, and the Zap does it without a person once it’s switched on. No run needed a human step in the middle to finish.

(c) The person in the loop. The person stays at the gate after the work, not inside it. The finance assistant reads each alert when it arrives and spot-checks it against the original email, and checks Zap History each evening for errored or missed runs. The agent may do one thing alone: notice an invoice email and send an alert to a fixed address. It may not reply to suppliers, forward invoices to anyone else, or decide anything about payment. It would move to A person leads, AI inside (top left) if it began checking invoice amounts against purchase orders before a manager approves payment, because then the person would have to sign off on what the AI checked. It would move toward A person decides, AI alongside (bottom right) if it started recommending which invoices to pay first, because that has no single right answer.

(d) My group’s view. In class, my group placed our School Mail Sentinel in AI alone, with checks. I agree, because like my agent it matches keywords, can only notify, and has no reply, forward or delete tool. On the question of whether the work is done together or alone, I would answer differently from my group for one task on our map, “Answer ‘where is my order?’ queries”, which the group placed in AI alone, with checks. That agent replies to customers directly, so a wrong answer reaches a customer before anyone checks it. My runs showed how easily a fixed rule goes wrong, with a missed “Inv.” email and one garbled alert, so I would treat it as work people and AI must do together, in A person leads, AI inside, until its error rate is known.

Reflection

My agent fitted the job well because spotting invoice emails is rule-based work with a right answer, so it could run alone with a person spot-checking alerts. It failed when a supplier wrote “Inv.” instead of “invoice”, which showed that a fixed search catches only the wording I predicted. I would widen the search further and add a weekly review of the inbox for invoices the agent missed, since a missed invoice costs more than a false alert.
